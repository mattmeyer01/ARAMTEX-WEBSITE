How to read Besco article numbers

A Besco article number tells you which line a fitting belongs to, its shape and its size. After this guide you will be able to read it without looking in the catalogue.

Number structure

Most numbers have four parts: the line prefix, the shape number, an optional suffix and the size after the hyphen. For example, GP5001-15 is a 90° i/a bend from the press V water line, diameter 15 mm.

Shape number

The last three digits of the group number indicate the shape and recur across lines. A 90° i/a bend is 5001 in solder, GP5001 in press V, 6001 in press M and G5001 in G-size.

In the solder and press V lines, a first digit of 4 instead of 5 usually indicates a threaded part, e.g. 4270 (fitting with female thread) alongside 5270 (coupling).

Letter suffixes

  • R: reducing version, e.g. 6130R, reducing tee in the M profile.
  • G: threaded version in the M profile and in steel, e.g. 6090G, 90° elbow with female thread.
  • S: slip version, e.g. GP5270S, slip coupling.

Size after the hyphen

  • -15: one diameter, 15 mm.
  • -28.12: several diameters separated by a dot, e.g. reducing coupling 28 × 12 mm.
  • -15.1/2: diameter and thread in inches, e.g. union 4331-15.1/2 is 15 mm × 1/2″.

Exception: in the press M lines (water and gas), the article numbers in the catalogue tables are long numeric codes, e.g. 682001212. On the group pages they appear in the tables together with the size, e.g. in group 6001.

Ordering by number

Enter the article numbers in the enquiry form or build a list in the product finder: open a line, e.g. press V, choose group GP5001 and add the diameters you need.

Data source: Besco Fittings & Connectors 2026 catalogue. As of 2026.
